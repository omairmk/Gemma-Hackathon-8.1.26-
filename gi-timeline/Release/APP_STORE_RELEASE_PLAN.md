It has no treatment-change workflow, Progress surface, or daily-completeness UI. A no-bowel-movement entry is dated. A person confirms a whole entry once before saving, saved entries are editable and may be marked for discussion, and selected entries can be exported as a readable PDF. Photos are local manual-entry attachments in this lane: the public interface does not analyze them, does not prefill fields from them, and does not claim on-device photo suggestions as a current feature.

GI Journal stores its live journal locally and requests exclusion from automatic device backup; iOS controls backup behavior. V1 provides no journal-transfer feature: it does not create a journal backup file or offer journal import or restore. Same-install unfinished-draft reopening and fail-closed journal retry remain local data-preservation safeguards. Deleting or reinstalling GI Journal, erasing, losing, or resetting the iPhone, or a storage failure may permanently lose entries and photos. A PDF is a readable, person-directed report and cannot restore the journal or create journal entries. There is no developer access to journal content.

## Current release promise

GI Journal is a private, local-first documentation tool. In the current public lane, a person creates a dated bowel-movement or no-bowel-movement Log entry, optionally attaches a photo, edits visible fields manually, confirms the whole entry once, and manages saved entries in Journal. Settings exposes the applicable local-data disclosure and erase controls. The current public route does not analyze photos and does not claim on-device photo suggestions as a shipping feature. There is no developer access to the person’s journal.

The public release has one commercial tier:

- The App Store purchase unlocks the complete app.
- There is no account, trial, subscription, consumable, non-consumable IAP, paywall inside the app, or server entitlement check.
- Manual logging, editing, saved data, and PDF export must never depend on model readiness or connectivity.
